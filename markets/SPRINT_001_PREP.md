## Learn → explain back → apply → challenge

Three short, movable encounters within the prospective approximately ten-day window; no dates or assumed hours. Keep the owner's output to one rough page or a map plus spoken explanation.

1. **Learn and explain back.** Before reading, briefly attempt: “Why would a business use a market, and who participates?” Save uncertainty rather than assuming a beginner baseline. Read the bounded primary excerpts, close them, and explain the need, investor motives, and risks in your own words. Familiar material moves quickly.
2. **Apply.** Read the case. Draw two panels: offering and hypothetical resale. Add money and ownership arrows. For each participant, explain its contribution, motive, and possible payment or benefit. Separate sourced facts, general reasoning, and unknowns. Include issuer, investor types, intermediary, venue, and relevant support infrastructure. Connect the picture to capital allocation, liquidity, price discovery, and risk-bearing without inventing unreported pricing mechanics.
3. **Challenge and repair, on a later encounter.** Answer below without sources. Then check disputed claims against the excerpts and correct your map, or name the precise gap. A model may challenge and give feedback after the attempt; it must not supply the owner's explanation.

Challenge prompts:

- Who receives the money on resale? Would a higher resale price automatically fund more company expansion?
- What if nobody buys at the holder's desired price? Why might this possibility matter to someone considering the original offering?
- Evaluate: “The exchange funds the business; the broker owns everything it helps sell; clearing prevents shareholders losing money.” Explain responsibilities and who still bears risk.
