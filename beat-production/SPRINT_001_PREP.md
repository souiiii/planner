## One small record

Make **one compact vocal demo, roughly 60–90 seconds**, over an existing beat you choose: a verse and short hook/refrain, with a deliberate beginning and ending. Write it yourself. The aim is to experience the whole recording and mixing loop on a manageable piece, not to perfect a full-length release.

Writing, delivery, recording, and especially vocal mixing receive the attention. Keep the beat largely as supplied; a stereo beat is enough. No beat-making, reference assignment, mastering project, or publishing task is included. Shortening the form is preferable to letting beat selection or arrangement consume the slice.

Use an available DAW that runs comfortably, the Razer Seiren Mini, and existing headphones. No DAW switch, new gear, or plugin purchase is a prerequisite. The reported gap is making convincing music, not learning software from zero.

## Make → bounce → listen → improve

Use these as movable work passes within the prospective approximately ten-day window, not scheduled sessions or a daily quota. Leave room for the vocal mix and one revision.

1. **Choose and write.** Choose a beat that makes you want to perform. Set the short form and finish a usable lyric. Try it aloud over the beat; revise phrases that impede delivery, then commit enough to record.
2. **Record and comp.** Make a short test take and listen before recording the piece. Capture the lead, retry weak phrases, and choose the strongest moments. Listen to the comp against the beat. Add doubles or ad-libs only if the song needs them; they are not a requirement.
3. **Process and mix; make a rough bounce.** Start from the actual voice and beat. Decide what sounds wrong and choose processing for that need. Work on intelligibility, consistency, and how the vocal sits with the beat. There is no prescribed chain or requirement to use every effect. If the weakness is in the writing or performance, revise or re-record instead of endlessly processing it. Export before continuing to tweak.
4. **Listen later and make one bounded revision.** Hear the bounce away from the editing session. Name the most distracting weakness, try a focused change, and export again. Keep both bounces. Stop after this revision; leave the next useful improvement for the next attempt rather than requiring a second record in this slice.

The owner chooses the beat, words, performance, processing, and artistic result. A model can help diagnose a specific reported problem after an attempt; it should not pre-write the song or design the vocal chain.
